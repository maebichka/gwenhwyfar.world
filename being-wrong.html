Blog > Notes on how to age gracefully, and the somatic feeling of being wrong

2026-11-20

Crossposted to Substack.

  • Hypothesis: for a supple, gracefully aging mind, learn to love being wrong, love the feeling of the world flipping, certitude dropping out from beneath your feet. it is a physical sensation ime.
    • ...but not too much, there's a ton of attractor states in that direction for a different kind of wrongness (eg never taking any stance at all)
    • This is probably also wrong
  • old people are annoying usually because they are stuck in their ways, but not just old people. Most people are pretty rigid to begin with, and definitely more stuck in their frame than they realize (including you, enlightened reader), and old age often just makes it painfully obvious and unhidable.
    • dementia personality (Parakeet Nebula)

  • the somatic feeling of realizing your entire frame might be, or is, wrong or incomplete
    • feels like the world dropping out from under you right past the top of a roller coaster
    • feels like looking down and the floor is glass and the ground miles below you
    • feels like the solid matter around you dissolving into mist
    • feels like taking something off a hook - first tighter, more uncomfortable - then a surprising amount of slack, freedom, breathing room, degrees of freedom. (I had something like this image in mind)
  • give up the sweet feeling of complete satisfaction, coherence, synthesis. you will give it up again and again and again.
  • you will never have certainty. sure, rest in certainty while you can. then, if you're paying attention, your world will get overturned. rinse and repeat. accept that this cycle happens without hope of resolution (Sasha Chapin/Bruce Tift)
  • learn to enjoy discombobulation
  • But, you do have to aim somewhere, in life. Pick a frame, any frame; they are all wrong. Wayfind from there

  • one type of flip: cultivating gratitude, when you are full of complaint
    • reverse all advice you hear. you may need the opposite of this. even if you think you don't, try the opposite on for size. try on both at the same time. try on neither.
  • but again, don't do it mindlessly/rigidly
    • human development goes something like: formlessness => certainty => uncertainty (repeat)
    • need to do/master/integrate BOTH, in transitions - do whatever the uncomfortable move is
      • but again....all universal laws are wrong (including this one). probably sometimes you need to do the comfortable move 🙃

  • this is the hardest thing for a human to do - our nature is to grasp. But don't worry, we've got our whole lifetime to practice: the point is NOT to settle on a fixed point, or even converging on one fixed target. In fact I would say this is """the point"""* of the project of human flourishing. You will never be done with this work. "Not stagnating" is a never-ending task. It does not halt. You will be comfortable sometimes, and uncomfortable other times, forever (or stagnate).
    • *"the point" of the project of human flourishing can be expressed in many ways; this is just one of them
  • This is antimemetic. It is smack in the middle of the Cringe mindfield.
    • Reduce the amount of possibility space that feels "forbidden" to you (from Charlie Awbery, via Andrew Conner).
  • Try on the opposite for size.

  • This, too, is wrong

  • PS: I started thinking about all the people who need the opposite of this advice*, and am now annoyed thinking about them taking this advice to heart. Do not listen to me!!!!
    • *those who see all sides but their own
    • *those who want everyone to get along and are hopelessly ineffectual for it
    • *those who believe in nothing
