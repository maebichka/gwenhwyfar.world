2026-11-22

Update: I mostly don't do this lol. the MVP version of this that i DO do though, when i go to a café or library to work, is:

  1. I bring my nice notebook with me.
  2. I open that before I open my laptop, and I write a list of things I want to do that day, maybe in order of easiness/satisfactoriness/importance.
  3. Then when I do use my laptop, my awareness is vaguely expanded to include the notebook list, which I check occasionally and helps me stay on task.

If your awareness is still chronically sucked into your laptop, you could still try using a timer to periodically check the todo list.

2026-09-18

  1. Before opening computer, write all computer tasks on a piece of paper. Split the list into tasks that can be done without internet, and those that can't.
  2. Minimize all as many windows as possible. Ideally do this at the end of any computer session, but otherwise do this at the start of a new session.
  3. Turn off your wifi and first complete the tasks that can be done without internet. Then turn on your wifi.
  4. Experiments
    • It may help to add a task to your test list that says something like "check texts/messages/Twitter" so that there is a discrete container in which to do those things. Put these tasks at the end of your list. You may also want to timebox them ("check Twitter for 10 minutes") and use a timer.
    • Every time you notice yourself getting sidetracked, write down what you were intending to do and what you ended up doing instead. It may be a long trail of unfinished open loops like "I started to draft an email but that reminded me that I had to order some medications so I went on the website and that reminded me that I wanted to message my friend" and then I opened Twitter to find a tweet to send her..."
      • in cases like this, I think the preferred behavior is instead to notice, "Oh, I have to order some medications," or "I want to message my friend about this tweet"; and instead of going to do those things immediately, zooming out a bit and taking a moment to add those things to your task list.
  5. If you're still struggling, find a timer online that rings repeatedly every 10 or 15 or 20 minutes. When it goes off, use the moment to expand your awareness, return to your list of tasks, and check in on what you are doing and what you want to be doing. The timer might seem extreme, but you can think of it as training yourself to eventually learn to do the check-in without a timer.
  6. Before you close your computer, minimize as many windows as possible.
